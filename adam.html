# The Story of Prophet Adam (AS) ### The First Human • The First Prophet • The Father of Humanity ## A Description of Adam (AS) Prophet Adam (AS) was the **first human being created by Allah (SWT)** and the father of all humanity. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ described Adam (AS) as being **sixty cubits tall**. Adam (AS) was created directly by Allah (SWT), from earth and clay. He was not born from a father or mother. Allah (SWT) says: > **“Indeed, the example of Jesus to Allah is like that of Adam. He created him from dust; then He said to him, ‘Be,’ and he was.”** > > — Qur’an 3:59 Adam (AS) was therefore a completely unique creation. He had no human parents. Allah (SWT) gave Adam (AS) a human form, gave him a soul, taught him knowledge, and honored him above many of His creations. From this one man, Allah (SWT) would bring forth an entire human race. --- # 1. Before Adam (AS) Before Adam (AS) was created, Allah (SWT) had already created many kinds of creation, including the **angels and the jinn**. Among the jinn was **Iblis**. Iblis worshipped Allah (SWT) and was present among the angels, but he was **not an angel**. He was from the jinn. Then Allah (SWT) announced something extraordinary to the angels: > **“Indeed, I am going to place upon the earth a successive authority.”** > > — Qur’an 2:30 The angels asked: > **“Will You place upon it one who causes corruption therein and sheds blood, while we declare Your praise and sanctify You?”** Allah (SWT) replied: > **“Indeed, I know that which you do not know.”** The angels were not rebelling against Allah (SWT). They were asking because they did not know the wisdom behind the creation of humanity. Allah (SWT) knew what they did not know. --- # 2. The Creation of Adam (AS) Allah (SWT) created Adam (AS) from the earth. The Qur'an describes Adam's creation using several descriptions, including **dust, clay, sticky clay, and sounding clay**. Allah (SWT) said: > **“And when your Lord said to the angels, ‘Indeed, I am going to create a human being from clay.’”** > > — Qur’an 38:71 Adam's (AS) body was formed. Then Allah (SWT) gave Adam (AS) life. Allah (SWT) said: > **“So when I have proportioned him and breathed into him of My spirit, then fall down to him in prostration.”** > > — Qur’an 38:72 The soul was created by Allah (SWT) and attributed to Him as an honor. This does **not** mean that Allah's (SWT) essence entered Adam (AS). Adam (AS) became the first human being. --- # 3. Allah (SWT) Gives Adam (AS) Knowledge Allah (SWT) then gave Adam (AS) something remarkable: **knowledge.** Allah (SWT) says: > **“And He taught Adam the names — all of them.”** > > — Qur’an 2:31 Allah (SWT) presented the names to the angels and showed them that Adam (AS) possessed knowledge that they did not have. Allah (SWT) asked: > **“Inform Me of the names of these, if you are truthful.”** The angels replied: > **“Glory be to You; we have no knowledge except what You have taught us.”** Then Adam (AS) named them. Allah (SWT) demonstrated Adam's (AS) special position and the wisdom behind his creation. --- # 4. Allah (SWT) Commands the Angels to Prostrate Allah (SWT) then commanded the angels: > **“Prostrate before Adam.”** The angels obeyed. Every one of them prostrated. But there was one exception. **Iblis refused.** Allah (SWT) asked him why. Iblis answered: > **“I am better than him. You created me from fire and created him from clay.”** > > — Qur’an 7:12 This was the beginning of Iblis's great rebellion. His problem was not that he did not know Allah (SWT). His problem was **pride and disobedience**. Allah (SWT) condemned him and expelled him from His mercy. But Iblis asked Allah (SWT) for something. He asked Allah (SWT) to give him respite until the Day of Resurrection. Allah (SWT) granted him respite. --- # 5. Iblis Makes a Promise Iblis then declared that he would try to mislead humanity. He said: > “Because You have put me in error, I will surely sit in wait for them on Your straight path.” > > — Qur’an 7:16 Iblis promised to approach human beings from different directions and tempt them away from Allah (SWT). But there was one group he knew he could not truly control: Allah's (SWT) sincere servants. Allah (SWT) made clear that Iblis would have no authority over those who sincerely followed Him. From that moment onward, human beings would face the whispers and temptations of Shaytan. --- # 6. Adam (AS) Enters Paradise Allah (SWT) then gave Adam (AS) a home in Paradise. Allah (SWT) said: > “And We said, ‘O Adam, dwell, you and your wife, in Paradise and eat therefrom in abundance wherever you will. But do not approach this tree, lest you be among the wrongdoers.’” > > — Qur’an 2:35 Adam (AS) had everything he needed. There was only one restriction: Do not approach the tree. It was a test of obedience. Allah (SWT) had given Adam (AS) many blessings, while asking him to stay away from one particular thing. --- # 7. Hawwa (AS) Is Created Allah (SWT) created Adam's (AS) wife, Hawwa (AS). The Qur'an describes humanity as originating from a single soul and its mate: > “O mankind, fear your Lord, who created you from one soul and created from it its mate and dispersed from both of them many men and women.” > > — Qur'an 4:1 Adam (AS) and Hawwa (AS) lived together in Paradise. They were allowed to enjoy its blessings. But they were warned about the tree. And Iblis had not forgotten his promise. --- # 8. The Whispering of Shaytan Iblis approached Adam (AS) and Hawwa (AS). He whispered to them. He tried to convince them that eating from the tree would give them something special. Allah (SWT) tells us: > “Then Shaytan whispered to them to make apparent to them that which was concealed from them of their private parts.” > > — Qur’an 7:20 Iblis swore to them that he was giving them sincere advice. He deceived them. Eventually, Adam (AS) and Hawwa (AS) ate from the tree. Immediately, they became aware of their nakedness. They began covering themselves with leaves from Paradise. --- # 9. Adam (AS) Makes a Mistake Adam (AS) had disobeyed the command of Allah (SWT). But there was a crucial difference between Adam's (AS) mistake and Iblis's rebellion. Adam (AS) did not become arrogant. He did not blame Allah (SWT). He did not insist that he was right. Instead, Adam (AS) and Hawwa (AS) recognized their mistake and turned back to Allah (SWT). They prayed: > “Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy upon us, we will surely be among the losers.” > > — Qur’an 7:23 This is one of the most important moments in the story of Adam (AS). Adam (AS) made a mistake. But he repented. And Allah (SWT) accepted his repentance. Allah (SWT) says: > “Then Adam received from his Lord words, and He accepted his repentance. Indeed, He is the Accepting of Repentance, the Merciful.” > > — Qur’an 2:37 This teaches humanity something incredibly important: Making a mistake does not mean a person is finished. A person can turn back to Allah (SWT). --- # 10. Adam (AS) and Hawwa (AS) Descend to Earth Allah (SWT) then told Adam (AS) and Hawwa (AS) that they would descend to Earth. Human life on Earth would become a test. Allah (SWT) said: > “Get down, all of you, from it. And when guidance comes to you from Me, whoever follows My guidance — there will be no fear concerning them, nor will they grieve.” > > — Qur’an 2:38 Adam (AS) was therefore not simply abandoned. He was being sent to Earth as part of Allah's (SWT) plan. Human beings would live, make choices, worship Allah (SWT), face Shaytan's temptations, and eventually return to Allah (SWT). --- # 11. Adam (AS) Becomes the First Prophet Adam (AS) was not merely the first human. He was also a prophet. Allah (SWT) guided him. Allah (SWT) taught him how to live and worship Him. Adam (AS) therefore became the beginning of the chain of prophets that would eventually include: Nuh (AS) Ibrahim (AS) Isma'il (AS) Ishaq (AS) Ya'qub (AS) Yusuf (AS) Musa (AS) Dawud (AS) Sulayman (AS) 'Isa (AS) Muhammad ﷺ Adam (AS) was the beginning of humanity's prophetic history. --- # 12. Adam (AS) and His Children Adam (AS) and Hawwa (AS) eventually had children. Humanity spread from them. Allah (SWT) says: > “O mankind, indeed We have created you from a male and a female and made you peoples and tribes that you may know one another.” > > — Qur’an 49:13 Every human being ultimately traces their ancestry back to Adam (AS) and Hawwa (AS). Among Adam's (AS) children were Qabil and Habil, commonly known as Cain and Abel. Their story became one of the earliest examples of jealousy, murder, regret, and the seriousness of taking a human life. --- # 13. The First Murder Allah (SWT) tells us about the two sons of Adam (AS). Both offered a sacrifice to Allah (SWT). One brother's sacrifice was accepted. The other's was not. The brother whose sacrifice was not accepted became jealous and angry. He said to his brother: > “I will surely kill you.” His brother replied: > “If you should raise your hand against me to kill me, I will not raise my hand against you to kill you. Indeed, I fear Allah, Lord of the worlds.” > > — Qur’an 5:28 But the angry brother killed him. This became the first murder among human beings. The murderer then did not know what to do with his brother's body. Allah (SWT) sent a crow that scratched into the ground, showing him how to bury his brother. The murderer said: > “Oh, woe to me! Have I failed to be like this crow and bury my brother?” He became regretful. But the terrible act had already happened. --- # 14. Adam's (AS) Life as a Father Imagine Adam's (AS) position. He was the first human being. There were no cities. No schools. No roads. No previous human civilization. No human ancestors before him. He had to learn how to live on Earth and raise the earliest generation of human beings. Adam (AS) taught his children about Allah (SWT). He taught them that Allah (SWT) alone deserved worship. He taught them right from wrong. He taught them about Shaytan. And he warned them about disobedience. Humanity began with a family that knew exactly where it came from: Allah (SWT). --- # 15. Adam's (AS) Long Life The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught that Adam (AS) was given a lifespan of **1,000 years**. Adam (AS) was once shown some of his descendants, and among them he saw **Dawud (AS)**. Allah (SWT) informed Adam (AS) that Dawud (AS) would live for **60 years**. Adam (AS) then gave **40 years of his own lifespan to Dawud (AS)**, making Dawud's (AS) lifespan 100 years. Later, when the angel of death came to Adam (AS), Adam (AS) said that he still had 40 years remaining. He had forgotten that he had given those 40 years to Dawud (AS). The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ mentioned this in an authentic narration, teaching us about human forgetfulness and the importance of remembering our promises. There is also an authentic narration about an encounter between Adam (AS) and Musa (AS). Musa (AS) mentioned Adam's (AS) mistake. Adam (AS) responded that Allah (SWT) had already forgiven him and that his descent to Earth had been decreed. The narration teaches important lessons about Allah's (SWT) decree, repentance, and human responsibility. Adam (AS) did not deny that he had made a mistake. Rather, he recognized that Allah (SWT) had forgiven him. --- # 16. Adam's (AS) Final Years Adam (AS) continued living his life on Earth. He worshipped Allah (SWT), taught his family, and guided his descendants. Generation after generation came after him. Humanity continued to grow. But eventually, the first human being would face the same reality that every human being faces: death. Allah (SWT) says: > “Every soul will taste death.” > > — Qur’an 3:185 Adam (AS), despite being the first human and a prophet, was not exempt from death. --- # 17. The Death of Adam (AS) Adam (AS) eventually died after his long life. The Qur'an does not give us a detailed description of Adam's (AS) final illness, final conversation, or exact burial place. There are later reports containing additional details, but those reports should not be treated as firmly established revelation unless they are authentically supported. What we know is that Adam (AS) eventually returned to his Lord. His earthly life had come to an end. The first human being had completed his journey on Earth. But his story would continue through every generation of humanity that followed. --- # 18. The Legacy of Adam (AS) Adam's (AS) story is much more than the story of the first human. It is also the story of every human being. Adam (AS) made a mistake. We make mistakes. Adam (AS) was tempted. We are tempted. Adam (AS) felt regret. We feel regret. Adam (AS) turned back to Allah (SWT). And we can turn back to Allah (SWT) too. The most important difference between Adam (AS) and Iblis was not that Adam (AS) never made a mistake. He did. The difference was what happened after the mistake. Adam (AS) admitted his wrongdoing and sought forgiveness from Allah (SWT). Iblis became arrogant and refused to repent. That distinction is one of the central lessons of Adam's (AS) story. --- # 19. What Adam's (AS) Story Teaches Us 1. Humans were created with purpose Allah (SWT) created Adam (AS) intentionally and with wisdom. 2. Knowledge is a great blessing Allah (SWT) specifically taught Adam (AS) knowledge. 3. Pride can destroy a person Iblis's arrogance led him to reject the command of Allah (SWT). 4. Shaytan is an enemy Iblis promised to mislead humanity, so believers are warned to be cautious of his whispers. 5. Everyone can make mistakes Adam (AS) was a prophet, yet he made a mistake. 6. Repentance matters Adam (AS) immediately turned back to Allah (SWT). 7. Allah (SWT) forgives sincere repentance Allah (SWT) accepted Adam's (AS) repentance. 8. Humans are responsible for their choices Adam's (AS) story teaches us that humans make choices and are accountable for them. 9. Earth is a test Human life includes temptation, difficulty, worship, choices, and ultimately death. 10. Death is not the end Adam's (AS) earthly life ended, but humanity's ultimate destination is the Hereafter. --- # The Beginning of Humanity And so the story of Adam (AS) begins with Allah (SWT) creating a man from clay. The angels were commanded to prostrate to Adam (AS). Iblis refused. Adam (AS) entered Paradise. He and Hawwa (AS) were tested. They made a mistake. They repented. Allah (SWT) accepted their repentance. They descended to Earth. Adam (AS) became the first prophet. Humanity grew. His children faced their first great trials. And after a long life, Adam (AS) returned to his Lord. From Adam (AS) and Hawwa (AS) came every human being who has ever lived. Every king. Every scholar. Every farmer. Every child. Every prophet. Every ordinary person. And every one of us. That is why Adam's (AS) story is not only the story of the first man. It is the beginning of our story. Allah (SWT) says: > “O children of Adam! Let not Shaytan tempt you as he removed your parents from Paradise…” > > — Qur’an 7:27 And Allah (SWT) gives humanity the same hope demonstrated in Adam's (AS) story: > “Indeed, He is the Accepting of Repentance, the Merciful.” > > — Qur’an 2:37 Adam (AS) was the first of us. And his story is a reminder that no matter how badly a person falls, the door of repentance to Allah (SWT) remains open. ← Back to Prophet Stories
